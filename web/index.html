Chapter 1

Once upon a coal mine

For decades, giant diggers pulled lignite out of the Cottbus-Nord mine. On 12 April 2019 the river Spree was let in.

Radar sees calm water as dark. Those first dark specks are the lake's very first puddles.

Chapter 2

The water learns to dance

Every 6 to 12 days a satellite passes over, and the dark patches spread. Separate pools joined into one lake in March 2023.

In September 2023 officials measured 5.9 km² of water. Watch the chart: our radar dot should land on their diamond.

Chapter 3

Full house

On 23 December 2024 the lake reached its target level of 62.5 m above sea level.

By February 2025 it covered 18.5 km², about 2,600 football pitches of brand-new lake.

Chapter 4

Six years in one picture

Every coloured pixel is lake. Its colour tells you the year it first went under water: violet for the first puddles, pink and gold for the middle years, aqua for the very last shores.

We only count a pixel once it stays wet on the next satellite pass too, so one noisy radar speckle can't fool us.

Chapter 5

NISAR keeps watch

The lake is full, so NASA and ISRO's new NISAR satellite takes the night shift. Its longer radar waves (about 24 cm) slip through leaves.

This is plant view: bright shores mean green life is moving back in. Drag the star: 2019 on the left, today on the right.

Chapter 6

Don't trust us. Check us.

The diamonds on the chart are official measurements by LEAG, the mine operator. Our radar dots should land right on them.

We've also faded the radar so you can see a regular photo from space underneath. Same lake, two very different eyes.
